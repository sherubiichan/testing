310MYA The earliest ancestors of mammals, known as synapsids, split from the reptilian and avian lineages.
Though only fragments have been discovered thus far, Protoclepsydrops (315MYA) may have been the earliest synapsid, but more fragments must be found and further research must be done for proper classification
Echinerpeton is one of the earliest discovered undisputed species in the synapsid lineage (308MYA in Nova Scotia, Canada)
Archaeothyris is another one of the earliest synapsids discovered (306MYA in Nova Scotia, Canada)
Predating the official Homo lineage, the oldest potential ancestor to start making and using tools (called "Lomekwi/Lomekwian" tools) came about at least 3.3MYA in modern day West Turkana, Kenya
Homo habilis was the earliest species we have so far identified in the hominid evolutionary line, living from about 2MYA to about 1.4MYA
Homo habilis is one of the hominid species associated with making and using "Oldowan" tools, named after the first place they were excavated in Olduvai Gorge, Tanzania, though they began to be produced before the earliest Homo species (about 2.6MYA-1.7MYA)
Findings of skulls showing traits of Homo habilis and Homo erectus from 1.8MYA in Dmanisi, Georgia, in the Caucasus Mountains challenge the popular theory that Homo erectus was the first hominid to leave Africa
An excavation at Ubeidiya in the Jordan Rift Valley uncovered a juvenile lumbar vertebra of an early Homo species from 1.2-1.6MYA
Homo erectus is still considered the first hominid species to emerge en masse from Africa starting at least 2MYA - reaching from modern-day Spain & Portugal and south England to the northwest, to East Asia and Indonesia to the east. Their fossils have been found as late as 110KYA before disappearing.
Homo sapiens in our modern physical form first appeared around 300-315KYA in Africa

The earliest findings of Homo sapiens so far were excavated in Jebel Irhoud, Morocco (315KYA)
